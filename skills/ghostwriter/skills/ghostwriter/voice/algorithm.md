# LinkedIn reach optimization (apply to every post)

Evidence-based, current as of **August 2026**. Claims are tagged: **[official]** = LinkedIn
statement or named exec; **[measured]** = named study with disclosed sample (Richard van der
Blom *Algorithm Insights* ~1.8M posts, AuthoredUp 621K+ posts, Originality.ai 3,368 + 5,000
posts); **[folklore]** = widely repeated, never demonstrated — listed only to stop it steering
decisions. **These rules must never override `voice-notes.md`** — where they conflict,
voice-notes wins.

## The AI-slop suppression is real — and the pattern is the tell

- **[official] Since May 2026 LinkedIn explicitly suppresses "AI slop."** Flagged posts are
  not deleted; they get **reduced distribution, confined roughly to the author's immediate
  network**. Targets: generic AI-generated posts/comments lacking original perspective,
  attention-bait, and **automation tools posting AI content at scale**. LinkedIn's stated
  bar: *"It's ok to use AI to help you write, but your posts and comments need to represent
  your voice."* Users also have a **"Seems like AI slop" report option**.
- **[measured]** AI-classified posts average **30–45% less engagement**; fully-AI-looking
  posts get ~2.8× less reach and ~5× less engagement (van der Blom 2025; Originality.ai 2025).
- **What gets a feed flagged is the pattern, not the punctuation.** No feature list is
  disclosed; claims that the classifier keys on em-dashes are [folklore]. What's targeted is
  *genericness, repetition, and velocity at the feed level*: uniform post length and shape,
  the same designed graphic on every post, near-daily cadence, a register unlike the
  author's own history. **Vary length, shape, visual, and cadence deliberately.** A feed of
  individually-good posts in an identical wrapper still reads as automation.
- The single most-cited human-perception tell is the **"it's not X, it's Y" construction**
  (already banned in voice-notes), followed by inspirational closers, emoji-bullet lists,
  formula hooks, and rule-of-three cadence. One recognized construction in the hook and
  readers treat the whole post as generated.

## The account is the unit, not the post

For a smaller account this outweighs every per-post rule in this file:

- A post is tested on a small slice (~2–5%) of your network; **early comment velocity in the
  first 30–90 minutes largely decides expansion** [measured trend]. Only ~5% of posts that
  underperform their first hour ever recover. ("70% of reach is decided in the first hour"
  is [folklore]; the direction is real, the number is not.)
- **[measured] The strongest lever a few-hundred-connection account has is the human's own
  commenting activity**: thoughtful 10+ word comments on 5+ target-audience posts around
  each publish measurably raise your own distribution, and replies within your own comment
  threads correlate with ~2.4× reach. Follower count itself is a weak signal; relevance-based
  ranking mildly *helps* small accounts producing specific content.
- **Publish-and-close-the-app guarantees a dead post.** If there isn't 10–15 minutes to
  engage after publishing, the correct move is to post later — or post less. Volume with
  zero engagement trains your own network to skip you, which gives every future test a
  worse start.
- Engagement bait ("Comment YES", "tag a friend") and AI-generated comments are actively
  suppressed [official].

## Recovering from a dead feed (the standing protocol while impressions sit under ~300)

Run this for ~3 weeks, then judge by the numbers:

1. **2–3 posts/week maximum**, weekday mornings only (see posting window below).
2. **Text-only or native screenshot** as the visual default; no designed card more than
   ~1 in 4 posts.
3. Recalibrated feed-native voice (voice-notes → Recalibration 2026-08-19).
4. **Before publishing, the engagement window is committed**: 10–15 min to reply to every
   comment and leave 5+ substantive comments on target-audience posts. No window → hold
   the post.
5. Record the **impressions number** for every post via the outcome check-in.
6. **Judge the trend across 6+ posts, never one post.** Platform-wide reach fell 25–28%
   year over year [measured], so a single small number proves nothing; a flat trend under
   the protocol means the diagnosis is wrong and the next lever is audience composition,
   not content.

## Engagement signals by weight — optimize for the top

- Ordering is reliable, exact ratios are soft: **comments and saves ≫ likes**. AuthoredUp
  has a save ≈ 5× a like and a comment ≈ 2×+ [measured]; "a comment is worth 15× a like"
  is [folklore].
- **Dwell time is treated as a primary quality signal** [measured-secondary]; ~30+ seconds
  is the meaningful threshold cited. Give the reader something to actually read or study —
  this is why decorative images do nothing and information-dense ones work.
- **For Nate: earn comments with genuine questions and saves with reference-worthy meat,
  never with CTA bait.** A real question you want answered is now a first-class ending
  (voice-notes → Recalibration); the reflexive forms stay banned.

## Bake into every draft

- **Hook in the first ~210 characters** — all that shows before "…see more". The most
  specific number or sharpest tension goes first. No throat-clearing.
- **Feed-native formatting**: one idea per line or a 1–2 sentence paragraph, blank line
  between, max paragraph ~40 words. Knowledge/advice content gets ~3–5× reach [measured];
  ~8th-grade reading level (denser than 10th grade ≈ 35% less reach).
- **Vary the length post to post.** Some posts are 40–60 words; a walkthrough can run
  longer. Twenty posts in a row at 100–250 words is a signature, and signatures of
  automation are what get suppressed.
- **No external links in the post body** — an in-body link costs ~60% reach [measured,
  very consistent]. **The link-in-first-comment workaround is reportedly detected and
  patched as of early 2026** [unverified] — don't advise it as free anymore; when a link
  matters, it still beats an in-body link, but weigh whether the post needs the link at all.
- **Hashtags are dead as a reach lever** [measured: zero impact over 8 months; >3 = ~70%
  *lower* reach]. Default none; ≤3 as a topic signal at most.
- **End on substance** — the last real point, or a genuine question (see voice-notes).

## Format, ranked by what earns dwell (the structural lever)

[measured] Personal-profile reach multipliers: documents/carousels 1.45× · images 1.18× ·
video 1.10× · text 0.88×. (Polls show 1.64× in the same dataset but are the least stable
claim in this space — 2026 reports conflict sharply; don't build on them.) But the
multiplier hides the mechanism, which is **information density earning dwell**:

1. **Info-dense carousel/document (PDF)** — highest engagement rate (~6.6%, optimal 8–12
   slides). The best format for a real how-to.
2. **Native screenshot or real photo** — [measured-secondary] real people/behind-the-scenes
   beat polished corporate visuals by up to 3×; a real terminal, a real chart, a real photo
   all qualify. Infographics (~2.4× average images) work because they carry information.
3. **Text-only with a strong hook** — beats a weak image on dwell and reads as a human,
   not a content pipeline. **The respectable default, and the recovery-protocol default.**
4. **A designed/decorative card is the weakest choice** — nothing to study, nothing earning
   dwell, and repeated identical branding across a feed is the "automation at scale"
   pattern [official target]. Use one only when the composition itself carries real
   information, and never as the routine wrapper.

## Posting behavior (Nate's actions — the skill prompts, the human does)

- **Cadence: 2–4×/week, never two posts within 24h.** [measured] 3–5/week is the optimum
  band; more than one per 24h splits the test-audience evaluation and hurts both posts.
  During recovery, hold to 2–3.
- **Posting window: weekdays ~7:00–13:00 local, Tue–Thu best.** This is a *default from
  aggregate data, not evidence about your audience* — refine it from your own impressions
  numbers as the outcome log fills in. Friday 10pm and Saturday 8pm are dead air for a
  professional audience.
- Don't delete-and-repost (it resets distribution).
- **Golden hour, concretely:** reply to every comment with substance (a question back, not
  "thanks"), and leave 5+ thoughtful comments on posts your target audience reads. This is
  the part no tool can do and the part the data says was skipped on all 20 posts to date.

## Honest caveat

Content optimization raises the ceiling; it can't manufacture an audience. If the recovery
protocol runs clean for 6+ posts and impressions stay flat, the constraint is audience
composition — the network doesn't contain the readers the content is for — and the fix is
sustained commenting/connecting in the AI-agent/DevOps builder community, not another
content tweak.
