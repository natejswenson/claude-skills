The bottleneck for AI in your operations isn't the model. It's everything your team knows and never wrote down.

The instinct, when agents get good, is to point them at your hardest problems and expect magic. Then they fall flat, and people decide the tech isn't ready.

Usually the tech is fine. The knowledge it needs just lives in three senior engineers' heads and nowhere else.

An agent can read every log line and dashboard you have. It can't read the thing your best responder simply knows: that this alert is usually noise, that the real fix is upstream of where the error shows up.

That judgment is your actual operational asset, and most of it has never been written down.

So the highest-leverage prep for AI in ops isn't a new tool. It's externalizing what your team already knows:

Write down why, not just the steps. The reasoning is what transfers.
Capture the dead ends. "We tried X, it looks right, it isn't" is gold.
Turn tribal knowledge into text your people and your agents can both read.

You're not documenting to replace anyone. You're making the knowledge that walks out at 5pm available at 2am.

The teams that win with agents won't be the ones with the best model. They'll be the ones who wrote down what they know.
